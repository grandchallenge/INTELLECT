# Decision proposal: make the semantic/mechanical boundary explicit

Problem: Article IX calls AETHER the coordination substrate, while the Council recommends an independently encapsulated mechanical plane. The broader word coordination can be misread as either excluding all external mechanics or transferring admission with transport. Neither reading establishes lawful powers.

Alternative A: retain existing text and use nonbinding commentary. Lowest migration cost and no constitutional change, but leaves the production naming ambiguity and cross-plane identity hazards implicit. Prefer A if E1 shows no need to amend normative wording.

Alternative B (proposed): add explicit ownership and non-authority rules to Article IX while preserving Articles X-XI and domain gates. This is reviewable without moving code. It makes mechanical influence attributable and preserves local AETHER correctness. Cost: a real amendment review and later synchronized authority-schedule validation.

Alternative C: make FABRIC a co-authoritative coordination substrate. Rejected: no admitted evidence establishes its right to order semantic history, interpret permissions or elect authoritative leaders. It also defeats the Council conditions and existing authority partition.

Discriminating evidence: compare E1 event/schema contracts with all 18 E0 seams; execute D1-D5 before any corresponding extraction. If an event can confer authority merely through delivery, scheduling or replication, revise B rather than reinterpret the test as success. If independent local AETHER operation cannot be retained, hold the split.

Scope: a candidate Article IX diff only. No effective Constitution/schedule or runtime file changes. Proposed 1.3.0 is a candidate identifier, not the currently effective version. Reversal before ratification is rejection or revision of this packet; after ratification requires the ordinary exact Human Steward process, never silent restoration of old law.
