# WP-GCT-GCL-MANDATE-003-MOE-FLUTTER — MoE Flutter Boundary E1 Reconnaissance

## Purpose

Determine whether sparse mixture-of-experts training systems possess a reproducible Hopf/flutter-like oscillatory instability boundary that is mechanistically distinguishable from ordinary expert collapse, stochastic router switching, optimizer-memory ringing, capacity clipping/drop periodicity, data/batch periodicity, measurement-window artifacts, or non-normal transient amplification.

## Scope

Pre-register and, only after applicable Charter/review gates clear, execute a bounded five-dimensional E1 reconnaissance over router learning rate, routing temperature, load-balancing gain, capacity factor, and optimizer-memory strength. The first work-package deliverable is the frozen experimental design: baseline/model/data identity, Sobol design, seed policy, observable vector, complex-mode estimator, classification rules, null tests, artifact contract, and resource-escalation trigger. Any later E2 surface nomination or E3 continuation must be based on predeclared selection rules and preserved negative evidence.

## Constraints

- The work package must not assume that a Hopf/flutter boundary exists.
- Ordinary expert collapse, router noise, optimizer ringing, capacity clipping, data/batch periodicity, measurement-window artifacts, and non-normal transient amplification are mandatory null explanations.
- The theoretical label `Hopf` remains provisional unless the mathematical conditions required for that classification are separately established.
- No material compute, A100/H100-scale campaign, external service, external publication, partnership, or product commitment is authorized by this Charter projection.
- E1 must use the smallest compute envelope capable of discriminating the declared null models; a larger envelope requires a bounded resource justification and applicable authorization.
- Analysis windows, mode-selection rules, classification thresholds, and candidate-surface nomination rules must be frozen before examining continuation results.
- Negative and null results must be retained as first-class evidence.
- Repository artifacts are a local authoring projection; production work-package authority remains subject to the INTELLECT Constitution and AETHER-backed history.

## Acceptance criteria

- `SPEC.md` pins a concrete reproducible MoE baseline/model/data configuration or a clearly identified minimal proxy plus an explicit later-scale validation boundary.
- `SPEC.md` defines numeric domains for all five control coordinates and a bounded Sobol/space-filling E1 design with a declared seed policy.
- The measurement contract covers router entropy, expert load/use, imbalance, capacity/drop/overflow, loss, gradients/updates, and timing/state variables required by the mode estimator.
- The mode-estimation protocol is frozen before execution and produces dominant complex eigenvalue/frequency/damping or growth estimates with window/uncertainty sensitivity checks.
- Explicit predeclared rules distinguish ordinary collapse, damped oscillation, persistent oscillation, and divergent instability.
- Null tests cover optimizer-memory ringing, capacity clipping/drop periodicity, data/batch periodicity, measurement-window sensitivity, stochastic router switching, and non-normal transient amplification.
- The E1 artifact contract is sufficient to reproduce every sampled point, seed, observable trace, fitted local model, classification, and candidate-surface score.
- A candidate surface may be nominated only under recurring controlled complex-mode behavior, coherent parameter dependence, cross-seed recurrence, observable regimes on both sides, and evidence against the declared simpler nulls.
- `SPEC.md` states an explicit resource-escalation trigger for any larger-GPU or materially larger run; no such escalation is implied by Charter admission.
- The return path allows `ACCELERATE`, `ACTIVE`, `NARROW`, `HOLD`, or `RETIRE`, and treats a negative result as a successful discriminating outcome.
